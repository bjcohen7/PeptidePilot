03 — The plan

Twelve months, three phases

This is sequenced deliberately. The single most common failure is starting everything at once, feeling awful, and quitting in week six — so nothing gets added until the thing before it is stable.

P1
Weeks 1–8 · Foundation

Establish the deficit without losing ground

GLP-1 initiation at the lowest dose, titrated on your prescriber's schedule — not on your impatience. Two things start the same week the pen does: a hard protein floor of 160 g/day, and three full-body lifting sessions a week. The training isn't optional garnish here. It is the actual mechanism that decides which of the two bars above you get.

EXPECT: 8–14 lb down · waist −1.5 to −2.5 in · strength flat or slightly up

P2
Weeks 9–28 · Recomposition

Add the second lever, change the scoreboard

Once GLP-1 tolerance is established, this is the window where a prescriber may consider adding a growth-hormone-releasing agent like tesamorelin, which has been studied specifically for visceral fat — the deep abdominal fat driving your 42-inch waist. Expect the scale to slow down and possibly stall. That is the plan working, not failing: you are adding lean tissue while subtracting fat. The tape measure becomes your primary metric in this phase.

EXPECT: 12–20 lb down · waist −4 to −6 in · visible change outpacing scale change

P3
Weeks 29–52 · Consolidation

Land it and keep it

Deficit narrows, calories come up, GLP-1 moves toward the lowest dose that holds appetite. Training volume goes up as recovery improves with the weight off your frame. This phase exists because the regain statistics on GLP-1 discontinuation are brutal when there's no plan for the exit — roughly two-thirds of lost weight returns within a year of stopping cold. Muscle you built in P1 and P2 is what makes the maintenance calories high enough to live on.

EXPECT: 193–198 lb · waist 34–35 in · ~15% BF · maintenance protocol defined

04 — The stack

What each piece is actually doing

Four components. Two of them require a prescriber, two of them are free and non-negotiable — and the free ones are doing more work than most people assume.

Component 01 · Prescription

GLP-1 / GIP receptor agonist tirzepatide · semaglutide

Job
Create and hold the calorie deficit. It slows gastric emptying and blunts the appetite signal, which is why it works where willpower reliably doesn't.
Why you
You reported a hunger score of 8/10 and evening eating as your main failure mode. This is the exact problem this class of drug solves.
Cadence
Weekly subcutaneous injection, dose titrated upward over months.
Watch for
Nausea and constipation during titration. Both are usually dose-rate problems, not permanent ones.
Rule outNot appropriate with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2, or with a history of pancreatitis. Your prescriber screens for this — but flag it yourself if it applies to you.
Component 02 · Prescription · Phase 2

Tesamorelin GHRH analog

Job
Stimulates your own pituitary to release growth hormone in natural pulses, rather than replacing it. The studied effect is a preferential reduction in visceral adipose tissue — the fat around organs, not the fat under skin.
Why you
A 42-inch waist at 238 lb points to a visceral-heavy fat distribution. That's the compartment most linked to metabolic risk, and it's the compartment this agent was studied against.
Cadence
Nightly subcutaneous injection before bed. Timing matters — GH release is sleep-dependent, which brings us to component 04.
Watch for
Injection-site reactions, joint stiffness, fluid retention. Glucose is monitored, since GH can nudge insulin resistance — relevant when you're on a GLP-1 at the same time.
StatusTesamorelin is FDA-approved only for HIV-associated lipodystrophy. Use for general body composition is off-label and entirely a prescriber's clinical judgment. Anyone selling it to you without that conversation is selling you a problem.
Component 03 · Free · Non-negotiable

Resistance training 3× / week, progressive

Job
This is the signal that tells your body which tissue to burn. Remove it and the deficit takes muscle and fat indiscriminately.
Why you
This single variable is what separates the two bars in the chart above. No peptide compensates for its absence. You've trained before — the neural pathways come back fast.
Cadence
Three full-body sessions. Compound lifts, 6–12 reps, add weight or reps every week you can.
Paired with
160 g protein daily — roughly 1 g per pound of lean mass. On a GLP-1 this takes planning, because you will not be hungry enough to hit it by accident.
Component 04 · Free · Currently your weakest link

Sleep 5.5 hr → 7+ hr

Job
Most GH release happens in slow-wave sleep. Tesamorelin amplifies a pulse that has to already be happening.
Why you
At 5.5 hours you are paying for a Phase 2 protocol and throwing away part of what it does. Short sleep also independently raises appetite and lowers training output — it's working against components 01 and 03 at the same time.
Cadence
Fix this in Phase 1, before the second injection ever enters the picture.

05 — Failure modes

The four ways this goes wrong

These are ranked by how often they end a protocol, not by how serious they sound.

Titrating up to chase the scale The scale stalls in week five, so you push the dose. Side effects spike, food intake craters, protein falls off, and you start eating your own muscle. The stall was normal. → Hold the dose. Move the tape measure instead.
Missing protein because you're not hungry The most common failure by a wide margin. A GLP-1 makes 160 g of protein feel like a chore, and the day you stop hitting it is the day the lean-mass bar starts shrinking. → Front-load it. 60 g before noon, while appetite suppression is weakest.
Substituting cardio for lifting Cardio burns calories you're already cutting with the medication and sends no signal to keep muscle. In a deficit, it can accelerate exactly the loss you're trying to prevent. → Lift three times. Walk daily. Keep hard cardio to a supporting role.
Starting both compounds in the same week If something goes sideways you have no idea which one caused it, and the honest answer is you'll probably stop both. Sequencing is a diagnostic tool, not caution for its own sake. → Eight weeks of stable GLP-1 before anything is added.
